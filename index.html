About

I am a Ph.D. candidate in the Department of Forest and Wildlife Ecology at the University of Wisconsin–Madison, advised by Prof. Min Chen in the Global Change Research Laboratory. I earned my bachelor's degree in Spatial Information and Digital Technology from the University of Electronic Science and Technology of China.

My doctoral research assesses the distribution, dynamics, drivers, and impacts of forest edges from regional to global scales by integrating multisource remote sensing and land-surface modeling. Alongside this, I am also exploring Antarctic greening, seasonal wetland mapping, mammal migration, and human adaptation to climate change.

Research

LiDAR for forest structure

Terrestrial, drone, and airborne laser scanning for tree extraction, leaf area index, gap fraction, canopy top height, and 3D canopy reconstruction.

Spaceborne remote sensing

Mapping vegetation structure and traits from imaging spectroscopy and radar, including PRISMA, NEON AOP, CYGNSS, and Sentinel-1.

Plant functional traits

Geospatial gradients of leaf and canopy traits across species and biomes, and the ecological strategies that shape their distribution.

Forest edges

Global and regional mapping of forest edges, their dynamics, disturbance drivers, and consequences for ecosystem function.

Forest hydrology
